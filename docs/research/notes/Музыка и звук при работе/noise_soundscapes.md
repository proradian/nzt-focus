# Non-musical background sound and focused cognitive work (noise, nature sounds, ambient soundscapes, office noise, binaural beats, "focus music")

Context: the focus-timer app plays Tabletop Audio soundscapes (fantasy/nature ambience plus light instrumental music, no vocals) during work blocks. The question is what the evidence says about ambient soundscapes and noise compared with silence.

Overall picture: no source found shows that ambient sound reliably beats silence for typical adults. The strongest positive evidence is (a) a small benefit of white/pink noise for people with ADHD or high inattention, (b) nature sounds, especially water, for restoration and mood, and for masking speech, and (c) one paper on moderate (~70 dB) ambient noise and creativity, which has not been replicated. The strongest negative evidence is that intelligible speech and changing-state sound are reliable distractors for verbal and memory work. Noise may slightly hurt attentive, typical people.

---

## 1. White, pink and brown noise: ADHD/inattention, stochastic resonance, typical adults, and the brown-noise trend

### Takeaway
White/pink noise gives a small, consistent benefit on lab attention tasks for people with ADHD or high inattention (pooled g ≈ 0.25). It gives a small harm for non-ADHD/attentive people (g ≈ −0.21). No controlled studies of brown noise met meta-analytic inclusion criteria. The brown-noise-for-focus claim is mostly a social-media trend extrapolated from white/pink-noise work.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigg, Bruton, Kozlowski, Johnstone & Karalunas (2024)**, *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 63(8):778–788 (USA, Oregon Health & Science Univ.). Systematic review and meta-analysis. Details:
  - **Samples.** 13 studies (12 white noise, 1 pink noise), N = 335 in the ADHD/high-symptom analysis. 11 child studies and 2 college-student studies (both unpublished theses). The studies came from Australia, Belgium, Canada, Taiwan, UK, US, Sweden and Norway.
  - **ADHD/high-symptom groups.** Pooled Hedges' g = 0.249 (95% CI 0.135–0.363), p < .0001. Heterogeneity was trivial (I² < 0.01).
  - **Non-ADHD/typical groups (k = 11).** Pooled effect was negative. The abstract gives g = −0.212 (CI −0.355 to −0.069). The results text gives g = −0.206 (CI −0.330 to −0.081). The difference between groups was significant: Q(1) = 28.0, p < .0001.
  - **Certainty and bias.** GRADE certainty was moderate. Blinding was impossible. No publication bias was detected. Six studies shared a senior or first author; excluding them left g = 0.228.
  - **Comparisons.** The authors compare the effect with stimulant medication (~0.7+) and say it is similar to other complementary interventions (~0.2–0.3).
  - **Brown noise and caveats.** No brown-noise studies were found. Interventions were brief, dB levels varied, and long-term hearing safety is unknown.
  - [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11283987/)
  - Note: the abstract and results report slightly different non-ADHD estimates, and both analyses list N = 335. This looks like a reporting inconsistency in the paper — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11283987/)
- **Söderlund, Sikström & Smart (2007)**, "Listen to the noise: noise is beneficial for cognitive performance in ADHD," *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* (Sweden).
  - Noise improved memory performance in the ADHD group and worsened it in controls.
  - The paper introduced the **Moderate Brain Arousal (MBA) model**: dopamine levels modulate how much noise is optimal. The proposed mechanism is **stochastic resonance**: moderate noise helps, while too much or too little hurts.
  - [PubMed 17683456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17683456/); [Lund Univ. record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/951355)
- **Söderlund, Sikström, Loftesnes & Sonuga-Barke (2010)**, *Behavioral and Brain Functions* 6:55 (Norway/Sweden/UK).
  - 51 secondary-school pupils did episodic verbal free recall with and without **white noise at 78 dB**.
  - Noise improved recall for inattentive pupils (teacher-rated, not clinically diagnosed) and worsened it for attentive pupils. It eliminated the recall gap between the two groups.
  - The authors asked for replication in larger samples with more noise levels.
  - [PMC2955636](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2955636); [HVL repository](https://hvlopen.brage.unit.no/hvlopen-xmlui/handle/11250/149359)
- **Söderlund et al. (2024)**, "Sensory white noise in clinical ADHD: Who benefits from noise, and who performs worse?" *Scandinavian J Child Adolesc Psychiatry & Psychology* 12:92–99. It examined individual differences within a clinical ADHD sample — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/e04e701f44bc4681a5551415e6f18c6e)
  - Secondary coverage says about one-third of ADHD participants performed worse with noise. This figure was not verified against the paper — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/white-and-pink-noise-show-promise-in-enhancing-attention-in-those-with-adhd/)
- **Brown noise:**
  - No brown-noise studies qualified for the 2024 meta-analysis — [Nigg et al. 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11283987/)
  - Consumer health sources describe focus claims for brown noise as mostly anecdotal — [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/adhd/brown-noise-adhd)
  - A commercial evidence guide grades brown noise for focus as "limited" and mostly extrapolated from white-noise work. It rates masking for sleep somewhat better, as "moderate". The source is a commercial site — [Superpower guide](https://superpower.com/guides/brown-noise-focus-sleep-evidence)
  - Several brown-noise "studies" quoted on blogs could not be verified.

### Inferences
- For a general-audience focus app, noise should be framed as an **optional, individual-difference tool**, not a universal booster. The best estimate for attentive adults is null to slightly negative.
- Brown noise is plausibly fine as a *masker* because it is steady-state and low-frequency. It has no specific evidence of being better than pink or white noise for concentration, so "brown noise boosts focus" should not be claimed.
- The evidence base is mostly children, short lab tasks and one research group, at moderate-to-loud levels (e.g. 78 dB). Generalising to adults doing hours of knowledge work is extrapolation.

### Gaps
- There are no large, preregistered RCTs in typical adults during real work.
- No head-to-head white vs pink vs brown comparison on cognitive tasks was found.
- The "≈1/3 of ADHD participants worse" figure in Söderlund 2024 was not verified from the full text.
- I found no reliable evidence on long-term use or hearing safety at the volumes used.

## 2. Moderate ambient noise and creativity (Mehta, Zhu & Cheema 2012) and replications

### Takeaway
A single multi-experiment consumer-research paper (USA/Canada) reports that ~70 dB ambient noise improves creative (abstract-thinking) performance compared with 50 dB, while 85 dB hurts. I found no independent direct replication. The paper is widely cited in "café noise" marketing.

### Cited Findings
- **Mehta, Zhu & Cheema (2012)**, "Is Noise Always Bad? Exploring the Effects of Ambient Noise on Creative Cognition," *Journal of Consumer Research* 39(4):784–799. Details:
  - **Design.** Five experiments.
  - **Results.** A moderate level (**70 dB**) improved creative performance compared with low noise (**50 dB**). It also increased purchase likelihood of innovative products. High noise (**85 dB**) hurt creativity.
  - **Proposed mechanism.** Moderate noise raises processing difficulty, which induces abstract processing. Loud noise reduces how much information people process.
  - [OUP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/39/4/784/1798283)
- Tasks included the **Remote Associates Test**. One experiment had 65 undergraduates. The noise was a blend of cafeteria chatter, roadside traffic and distant construction — [Univ. of Illinois press release, via therapytoronto](https://therapytoronto.ca/news/2012/05/study-suggests-ambient-background-noise-has-significant-effect-on-consumers-creativity/); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/06/120619230057.htm)
- The press coverage was picked up by coworking and productivity media and used to justify café-noise apps — [deskmag](https://deskmag.com/en/ambient-noise-boosts-creativity-coworking-spaces-493)
- A 2019 *Frontiers in Psychology* paper builds on Mehta et al. but is not a replication — [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00381/full)

### Inferences
- The effect is about **creative or divergent tasks**, not convergent, detail-oriented or verbal-memory work. For the latter, the irrelevant-speech literature (section 4) predicts that café chatter hurts when the speech is intelligible.
- 70 dB is fairly loud for headphones over long periods. App defaults should be lower. The useful message is "moderate, not loud".
- The creativity stimulus contained speech babble. Tabletop Audio-style soundscapes without speech are a different stimulus, so the finding does not transfer directly.

### Gaps
- I found no independent replication, failed replication or meta-analysis of the 70 dB creativity effect. Given the replication-crisis era of the original, this is an important gap.
- Per-experiment sample sizes and effect sizes beyond the 65-participant experiment were not retrieved.

## 3. Nature sounds: restoration, mood, stress, cognitive performance, and masking while working

### Takeaway
Nature sounds, especially water and birdsong, have reasonably consistent evidence for **stress recovery, mood and post-exposure restoration of directed attention**. The evidence comes from a PNAS synthesis of 18 studies and several experiments. The evidence for nature sounds **played during** work is thinner: a few small studies and a conference abstract. The best-supported working use is **masking intrusive speech** with water-like sound.

### Cited Findings
- **Buxton et al. (2021)**, "A synthesis of health benefits of natural sounds and their distribution in national parks," *PNAS* (Canada/USA: Carleton Univ., Colorado State, Michigan State, US National Park Service). Details:
  - **Design.** A meta-analysis of **18 publications**.
  - **Findings.** Natural sounds were associated with lower stress and annoyance, better mood, less pain and **improved cognitive performance**.
  - **Sound types.** **Water sounds** had the largest positive health effects. **Bird sounds** had the largest effect on stress relief.
  - **Mixed soundscapes.** Natural sounds mixed with anthropogenic noise still outperformed traffic noise alone.
  - **Sources.** These details come from press summaries, not the PNAS full text — [Carleton Univ.](https://carleton.ca/biology/2021/prof-rachel-buxtons-research-finds-nature-sounds-can-alleviate-stress-improve-mood-and-enhance-cognitive-performance/); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/science/nature-sounds-improve-health/)
  - A Carleton news item says outcomes were "180 per cent better". This is probably a misreported effect size and is unverified — [Carleton newsroom](https://newsroom.carleton.ca/story/nature-sounds-alleviate-stress)
- **Van Hedger et al. (2019)**, "Of cricket chirps and car horns: The effect of nature sounds on cognitive performance," *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 26(2):522–530 (USA, Univ. of Chicago).
  - Participants did a backward digit span plus dual n-back composite before and after listening to (and rating) nature or urban soundscapes. Each participant heard only one type.
  - The nature group improved more on directed-attention tasks, with no mood difference.
  - [PubMed 30367351](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30367351/); summary of no mood effect in [Sci Rep 2022 follow-up](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-20841-0)
  - **Important:** this is a *restoration* design. Sounds came between tasks, not during them.
- **Sci Rep (2022) follow-up**: birdsong vs traffic noise on mood, paranoia and cognition, online (Germany) — [Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-20841-0). Detailed effect sizes were not retrieved.
- **Benfield, Taff, Newman & Smyth (2014)**, "Natural sound facilitates mood recovery," *Ecopsychology* (USA).
  - Mood was lowered with disturbing videos. Then participants heard natural sounds, natural plus human-made (vehicle, voice) sounds, or other conditions.
  - Natural-only soundscapes produced better mood recovery than the same soundscape with anthropogenic noise.
  - Details are from journalistic summaries; the original was not accessed — [Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/nature-sounds-independent-beautiful-scenery-helps-boost-your-mood-306745); citation confirmed in [PMC11602051 reference list](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11602051/)
- **Gilmour et al. (2024)**, *PLOS ONE*, "Natural soundscapes enhance mood recovery amid anthropogenic noise pollution." Traffic noise, especially at higher speeds, masked the mood benefits of birdsong — [PMC11602051](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11602051/)
- **Annerstedt et al. (2013)**, "Inducing physiological stress recovery with sounds of nature in a virtual reality forest — Results from a pilot study," *Physiology & Behavior* 118:240–250 (Sweden/Denmark).
  - After a VR stress test, **only the virtual-forest-with-nature-sounds group** showed parasympathetic activation, a marker of stress recovery.
  - The forest-without-sound and control groups showed no notable change. This suggests that sound, not just the visuals, drives the effect.
  - [PDF](https://blogs.ubc.ca/2017wufor200/files/2017/01/Inducing-physiological-stress-recovery-with-sounds-of-nature-in-a-virtual-reality-forest-Annerstedt-et-al-2013.pdf); [Univ. of Copenhagen record](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/da/publications/virtual-nature-environment-with-nature-sound-exposure-induce-stre)
- **DeLoach, Carter & Braasch (2015)**, "Tuning the cognitive environment: Sound masking with 'natural' sounds in open-plan offices" (USA, Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute).
  - This is an **Acoustical Society of America meeting presentation (169th meeting, Pittsburgh) plus a master's thesis**, not a confirmed JASA journal article.
  - **N = 12.** Conditions were conventional electronic masking, a natural masker (mountain-stream-like flowing water) and no masker.
  - Press reports say the natural masker improved mood and productivity. This is **weak evidence**: tiny N, not peer-reviewed as a full paper.
  - [ASA lay-language paper](https://acoustics.org/2pnsa3-tuning-the-cognitive-environment-sound-masking-with-natural-sounds-in-open-plan-offices-alana-g-deloach-jeff-p-carter-and-jonas-braasch/); [RPI thesis record](https://dspace.rpi.edu/items/46cefae7-c9c8-4216-bc5f-1426df78841e)
- **Haapakangas et al. (2011)**, *Acta Acustica united with Acustica* (Finland). **N = 54.**
  - Short-term memory was worse with speech than in silence, and also worse with most masked-speech conditions.
  - **Only a spring-water sound improved performance relative to unmasked speech.**
  - [Univ. of Turku thesis summary](https://www.utupub.fi/handle/10024/135298)
- **Jahncke et al. (2011)**, *Journal of Environmental Psychology* (Sweden, Univ. of Gävle). After noisy office work, watching a nature film with river sounds raised self-rated energy more than continued office noise (see section 4) — [abstract on academia.edu](https://independent.academia.edu/NiklasHalin)

### Inferences
- The best-evidenced role for nature sound in a focus app is **(1) during breaks**, as restoration between Pomodoro blocks, and **(2) as a speech masker** in noisy environments. For water sounds as a masker there is at least one controlled study (Haapakangas 2011).
- Natural soundscapes "contaminated" by human-made sound (traffic, voices) lose benefit (Benfield 2014; Gilmour 2024). This argues for curating Tabletop Audio tracks without crowd chatter, taverns full of voices or city noise for work blocks. Those could be kept for breaks or labelled "busy".
- Fantasy ambience with **instrumental music** is not the same stimulus as pure nature sound. Melodic, changing-state music elements may carry irrelevant-sound costs for verbal or serial tasks (section 4) that pure water or rain do not.

### Gaps
- Per-study effect sizes and sample sizes for Buxton 2021, Van Hedger 2019 and Annerstedt 2013 were not retrieved from full texts.
- I found almost no well-powered studies of nature soundscapes played **continuously during** sustained knowledge work, as opposed to before and after.
- Benfield 2014 was not accessed in the original. DeLoach 2015 does not appear to exist as a full peer-reviewed article.
- I found no study testing fantasy or "RPG" ambient soundscapes (Tabletop Audio-type) on cognition.

## 4. Open-plan office noise: intelligible speech is the key distractor (Jahncke; Haapakangas; Hongisto STI model)

### Takeaway
Two literatures from Finland, Sweden and Germany converge: **intelligible irrelevant speech**, indexed by the Speech Transmission Index (STI), and other **changing-state** sound are what impair verbal short-term memory, working memory and reading. Steady-state noise usually does not. Effects are task-dependent: verbal and semantic memory tasks are vulnerable, while word fluency and some math tasks are not.

### Cited Findings
- **Jahncke, Hygge, Halin, Green & Dimberg (2011)**, *Journal of Environmental Psychology* (Sweden). Details:
  - **Sample.** **N = 47** students in a simulated open-plan office.
  - **Conditions.** High noise (**51 dB LAeq**) vs low noise (**39 dB LAeq**), followed by four restoration conditions.
  - **Results.** The high-noise condition led to **worse recall and more fatigue**. A nature film with river sounds increased self-rated energy more than office noise did.
  - [abstract via academia.edu](https://independent.academia.edu/NiklasHalin)
- **Hongisto (2005) STI–performance model.** Performance declines as speech intelligibility (STI) rises.
  - The Haapakangas (2017) doctoral thesis combined 3 lab experiments and 2 field studies. It found that STI predicts subjective disturbance and performance in **verbal short-term and working-memory tasks**, consistent with Hongisto's model — [Univ. of Turku](https://www.utupub.fi/handle/10024/135298)
- **Jahncke, Hongisto & Virjonen (2013)** (Sweden/Finland). The shape of the STI–performance curve and the size of the decrement **depend on task type**.
  - A word-fluency task was unaffected by speech intelligibility.
  - The steepest performance drop came at a lower STI than Hongisto predicted.
  - [Euronoise 2018 paper summarizing](https://www.euronoise2018.eu/docs/papers/316_Euronoise2018.pdf); [Univ. of Turku record](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/31562068?lang=en_US)
  - One re-analysis put the steepest decline between **STI 0.23 and 0.34** — [Euronoise 2018](https://www.euronoise2018.eu/docs/papers/316_Euronoise2018.pdf)
- **Haapakangas, Hongisto & Liebl (2020)**, "The relation between the intelligibility of speech and cognitive performance – A revised model based on laboratory studies," *Indoor Air* 30:1130–1146 (Finland/Germany). The model was revised using pooled laboratory data. The citation was confirmed, but the detailed parameters were not retrieved — [Turku UAS publication list](https://akustiikka.turkuamk.fi/uploads/2022/11/2be00f0a-publications-2016-to-2021-25nov2022.pdf)
- **Changing-state vs steady-state sound (the irrelevant sound effect):**
  - Changing-state sound disrupts serial recall more than steady-state sound, and steady-state sound often has no effect at all (Jones & Macken 1993) — [PMC7381464](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7381464/)
  - **Schlittmeier, Hellbrück & Klatte (2008)** (Germany): **instrumental staccato music** impaired serial recall compared with legato music and silence. The changing-state speech effect was replicated against steady-state speech, **pink noise** and silence — [PMC7381464](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7381464/)
- **Reading meta-analysis (Bournemouth Univ.; to my knowledge Vasilev, Kirkby & Angele 2018, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*).**
  - Background noise, speech and music all have a **small but reliable detrimental effect on reading**.
  - **Intelligible speech and lyrical music** are the most distracting.
  - [Bournemouth eprint](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/29995/); summary via [search-result abstract, see also PMC7381464](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7381464/)
  - The exact effect size was not verified.

### Inferences
- The app's no-vocals rule is well supported. Lyrics and speech are the most consistently harmful background elements for verbal and reading work.
- Within Tabletop Audio tracks, **voices, crowd murmur and tavern chatter** behave like irrelevant speech, especially if partly intelligible. **Sharply changing or staccato melodic elements** carry changing-state risk. **Steady rain, wind, water and drones** behave more like steady-state maskers, which are relatively harmless and can mask office speech.
- A practical rule for users in noisy offices or cafés: a steady masker (water, rain, pink noise) at modest volume is evidence-based for **reducing speech intelligibility**. The masker is not good in itself; it removes the worse distractor.

### Gaps
- The full numeric parameters of the 2020 *Indoor Air* revised model (e.g. performance-decrement % at given STI) were not retrieved.
- The Haapakangas & Hongisto field study of 21 offices was not summarized (text not accessible).
- I found no direct experiments on ambient fantasy soundscapes with sparse instrumental music vs silence on reading or writing.
- The Kämpfe et al. (2011) background-music meta-analysis (*Psychology of Music*) was not verified in this search.

## 5. Binaural beats: meta-analyses and strength of evidence

### Takeaway
One meta-analysis (Garcia-Argibay et al. 2019) reports a medium pooled effect (g = 0.45) across cognition, anxiety and pain. Exposure before the task works better than during it. Later work has not held up well: a three-experiment test found no attention benefit, and a 2023 systematic review found most EEG studies contradict the entrainment hypothesis. The evidence is weak to mixed, and binaural beats should not be marketed as a focus booster.

### Cited Findings
- **Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales (2019)**, "Efficacy of binaural auditory beats in cognition, anxiety, and pain perception: a meta-analysis," *Psychological Research*.
  - **22 studies, 35 effect sizes.** Overall **g = 0.45**, described as medium, significant and consistent.
  - Exposure **before**, or before plus during, the task worked better than exposure **only during** the task. Exposure time contributed significantly.
  - [PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/)
  - An attention-specific weighted effect of ~.58 is quoted second-hand by Robison et al. 2022 and was not verified in the original — [PubMed 33978805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33978805/)
- **Robison et al. (2022)**, "The effect of binaural beat stimulation on sustained attention," *Psychological Research* (USA). Across **three experiments**, there was **no consistent evidence** that binaural beats improve sustained attention or its subjective and physiological correlates — [PubMed 33978805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33978805/); [AZ Regents record](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/the-effect-of-binaural-beat-stimulation-on-sustained-attention/)
- **Ingendoh, Posny & Heine (2023)**, *PLOS ONE*, systematic review of 14 EEG studies (Germany).
  - **5 supported** brainwave entrainment, **8 contradicted** it and 1 was mixed.
  - Methods were highly heterogeneous, so the research question "cannot be settled".
  - [PMC10198548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10198548/)
- **Basu & Banerjee (2023)**, *Psychological Research*: mixed results for attention and memory — [via search summary, PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/)
- Commercial binaural-beat promotions (e.g. "Einstein brain" audio programs) circulate as press releases, not research — [WBOC press release](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/press_releases/neuro-energizer-reviews-einstein-brain-audio-program-evaluated/article_a38978b3-46e1-59f5-82f0-cc9f654f764d.html)

### Inferences
- Because the 2019 meta-analysis found **during-task** exposure weaker, binaural beats during a work block are the least-supported use case.
- If the app ever adds binaural beats, they require headphones by definition, should be labelled experimental, and should not carry efficacy claims.

### Gaps
- The domain-specific pooled effects from Garcia-Argibay 2019 (attention vs memory) and the publication-bias analysis were not verified from full text.
- The sample size and country details for Basu & Banerjee 2023 were not retrieved.

## 6. Commercial "focus music" claims (Brain.fm, Endel)

### Takeaway
**Brain.fm** has one peer-reviewed paper (Woods et al. 2024, *Communications Biology*) co-authored by company employees. It shows neural effects of fast amplitude-modulated music and an ADHD-symptom-moderated behavioral effect, but **no clear overall behavioral advantage over pink noise**. **Endel**'s "7x focus" claim rests on a company-commissioned study (Arctop) described in its own blog as a white paper. Both carry conflict of interest and lack independent replication. They should be treated as marketing-adjacent.

### Cited Findings
- **Woods, Loui, Morillon et al. (2024)**, "Rapid modulation in music supports attention in listeners with attentional difficulties," *Communications Biology* (USA/France).
  - **Affiliations:** Brain.fm, Wesleyan, Northeastern, Boston Univ., Aix-Marseille Univ.
  - **Conflicts:** first author K.J.P.W. is a **Brain.fm employee with equity options**, a second author was a Brain.fm employee with equity, and Brain.fm produced the music.
  - **Funding:** NSF STTR award (a small-business tech-transfer grant).
  - [PMC11499863](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11499863/)
  - Experiments and results:

    | Experiment | Sample | Conditions | Result |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1 | Online MTurk, SART task; N = 83 analysed of 160 recruited (~52% passed screening) | Fast-AM music, slow-modulation control music, pink noise | The music × block interaction was driven by an early-presentation effect of AM music |
    | 2 | fMRI; N = 34 Wesleyan undergraduates | Same three conditions | Higher attention/salience-network activation for AM music than for the other two conditions, including pink noise |
    | 3 | EEG; N = 40 | Same conditions | Stronger stimulus–brain coupling at modulation rates |
    | 4 | Online MTurk; N = 175 analysed of 221 recruited | Modulation rate (8/16/32 Hz) and depth; **no pink-noise condition** | Rate × block × ASRS interaction, p = .048, ηp² = .048. Depth had no effect |

  - The Discussion says the 16 Hz (beta) benefit was for **high-ADHD-symptom (ASRS)** listeners, but one Results sentence says the opposite. The paper does not resolve this.
  - No clear overall behavioral superiority over pink noise is reported. [PMC11499863](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11499863/)
  - An author correction (Sept 2025) added an author — [PMC12420781](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12420781/)
  - Coverage of the study is dominated by Brain.fm's own blog — [Brain.fm blog](https://www.brain.fm/blog/does-brain-fm-actually-work). There is also independent lay coverage — [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/background-music-amplitude-modulation-adhd-study/)
- **Endel:**
  - The company cites a 2022 study by **Arctop**, a neurotech company, using EEG during four one-hour sessions comparing Endel, focus playlists and silence. This is the source of the "7x better focus" claim.
  - Endel's own blog calls this a **white paper**, and publication dates are inconsistent across company materials.
  - [Endel blog](https://endel.io/blog/the-science-behind-focus); [Endel science page](https://www.endel.io/science); [Amazon Science piece](https://amazon.science/latest-news/how-endels-ai-powered-focus-soundscapes-earned-the-backing-of-neuroscience)
  - No independent replication or sample size was found.

### Inferences
- There is **no independent, peer-reviewed evidence that any commercial focus-audio product beats silence or plain pink noise on real work output** for typical adults.
- The app should avoid comparative efficacy claims ("scientifically proven focus") for Tabletop Audio soundscapes. Defensible framing: "ambient, speech-free sound that can mask distractions and make work blocks more pleasant; some people focus better in silence."

### Gaps
- No independent replication of Woods et al. 2024 was found, and Experiment 1/2 behavioral effect sizes for AM music vs pink noise were not clearly reported.
- No peer-reviewed Endel study with retrievable methods or N was found. The Frontiers/bioRxiv items linked from Endel's press release were not checked.
- No studies of café-noise apps (e.g. Coffitivity) or ambience apps on real-world productivity were found.
