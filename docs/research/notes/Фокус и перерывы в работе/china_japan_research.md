# East Asian (China / Japan) Research and Guidelines on Focus Duration, Mental Fatigue, and Work Breaks

Scope note: ~17 searches/fetches (Oct 2026). Japanese sources were richer and more official; Chinese sources are mostly lab/EEG and epidemiological nap studies, and no current Chinese national standard specifying computer-work break intervals was found. South Korea / Taiwan were not covered (time budget). Evidence strength flagged inline: [STRONG] = official guideline or large cohort; [MODERATE] = controlled experiment, small n; [WEAK] = n < 15, observational, secondary citation only, or unverified.

---

## Q1. Japan: MHLW VDT (2002) and information-equipment work guideline (2019) — the 60 min / 10–15 min rule and its scientific basis

### Takeaway
Japan's MHLW guideline (2002 VDT guideline, carried over unchanged into the 2019 「情報機器作業における労働衛生管理のためのガイドライン」) says continuous screen work should not exceed 60 min, followed by a 10–15 min break, with 1–2 short pauses (小休止, ~1–2 min) within each 60-min block. It is an administrative "good practice" rule. No primary study establishing the 60-min figure was found; it matches US (1997) and UK (HSE) guidance of the same period and is supported mostly by small studies on eye strain and well-being.

### Cited Findings
- **The rule (2019 guideline):** MHLW issued the guideline on 12 July 2019 (令和元年7月12日), renaming and replacing the 2002 VDT guideline. For information-equipment work: 一連続作業時間 ≤ 1 hour; 10–15 min 作業休止 before the next continuous work block; 1–2 小休止 inside each continuous block. [STRONG, official] — [MHLW leaflet 000658237](https://WWW.mhlw.go.jp/content/000658237.pdf); [MHLW guideline text 000539603](https://WWW.mhlw.go.jp/content/000539603.pdf); [Okayama Univ. leaflet](https://www.okayama-u.ac.jp/user/hokekan/leaflet.pdf)
- **Who it applies to:** Work categories. The time-management measures are "set" (設定) for category A and "guided" (指導) for category B. Category A covers ≥4 h/day of work with constant display watching where workers can't easily choose when to rest. — [MHLW 作業区分 table 000528420](https://WWW.mhlw.go.jp/content/000528420.pdf)
- **2002 VDT guideline wording:** For 「単純入力型」 and 「拘束型」 work: continuous work ≤ 1 h; 10–15 min 作業休止 between blocks; 1–2 小休止 within a block. The 2019 revision did not change the break concept. A note in the new guideline cites unnamed research suggesting that **~300 min (5 h) per day** of such work is desirable. The 2019 version also adds tablets and smartphones, while admitting evidence on them is limited. — [労働新聞社 FAQ](https://www.rodo.co.jp/faq/76700/); [Kagawa Labour Bureau page](https://jsite.mhlw.go.jp/kagawa-roudoukyoku/riyousha_mokuteki_menu/shurouchu/roudousya/5/5-5.html) (also says to keep daily continuous VDT time short)
- **Duration of 小休止:** Fujita et al. (2019, J Occup Health) read the MHLW 2002 rule as 1–2 short breaks of **1–2 min** each within the hour. — [Fujita et al. 2019, PMC6499340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499340)
- **International alignment:** As summarized by Fujita et al. (2019): US Dept. of Labor (1997) recommends 15 min rest after 1 h of high-visual-demand/repetitive VDT work, or 10 min after 2 h of moderate demand. UK HSE (1992) sets no fixed rule but says short frequent breaks beat long rare ones (e.g., 5–10 min after 50–60 min beats 15 min every 2 h). The 20-20-20 eye rule is also cited. Under the Japanese rule, breaks take ~13.8–21.3% of a 4-h shift, vs. ~4.2–18.8% under the US guidance. — [PMC6499340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499340)
- **Empirical support cited in Japan:** Balci et al. (2003; not Japanese) compared 60/10, 30/5, and 15-min work + micro-breaks. The **15-min + micro-break** schedule gave the least eyestrain/blurred vision and the best speed and accuracy. — cited in [PMC6499340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499340)
- **Japanese field study, "blind working" (Fujita et al. 2019, Japan, n=10 office workers):** In a 20-min window, workers spontaneously closed their eyes 7.4 ± 4.7 times, for 8.6 s on average (~4.8% of time). Compared with normal work, closing the eyes during work lowered ocular fatigue VAS (54.3 → 29.1, P=0.004), dry eye (49.7 → 29.1), and blurred vision (39.6 → 26.5). More eye-closure time correlated with less ocular fatigue (ρ = −0.694). The authors note that no guideline addresses such micro-pauses. [WEAK: n=10] — [PMC6499340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499340)
- **Nagasaki Univ. thesis (2007, Japan):** Long VDT work was associated with poorer mental health, and taking 小休止 was negatively associated with mental strain. [WEAK: observational, thesis abstract] — [Nagasaki Univ. PDF](https://www.nagasaki-u.ac.jp/ja/gakusai/summary/h18/0703_ishi_i/file/ishi-i102ronbun.pdf)
- **Compliance is low:** In an MHLW survey, only **11.1%** of workplaces ran VDT work-time management measures. Of those, **43.4%** provided the 10–15 min break. — [MHLW 技術革新と労働に関する実態調査, 第9表](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/toukei/itiran/roudou/saigai/anzen/03/hyou09.html)

### Inferences
- The 60/10–15 rule is mainly about **visual and musculoskeletal load** (eye strain, posture) during "constrained" data-entry work, not cognitive focus. It is a precautionary consensus value rather than an empirically optimized number.
- For a focus app, a ~60-min session followed by a 10–15 min break, with 1–2 micro-pauses (≈1–2 min, or even brief eye closures) inside the hour, maps directly onto the Japanese official rule. That makes it defensible to describe as "aligned with Japan's MHLW guideline."
- The evidence that does exist (Balci 2003, Fujita 2019) points to **more frequent micro-breaks** improving comfort and even performance. This argues for building micro-pause prompts inside the 60-min block.

### Gaps
- The original scientific basis for "60 min" in the 2002 MHLW guideline (e.g., which 1980s–90s Japanese studies the expert committee used) was not found. The PDF of the guideline commentary could not be text-extracted.
- The study behind the "300 min/day" note is unnamed in the secondary source.
- No Japanese occupational-health-society (産業衛生学会) paper testing 60/10 vs. alternative schedules was located.

## Q2. Japan: JNIOSH and Japanese research on rest breaks, short naps, mental-fatigue measurement, inemuri

### Takeaway
Japanese research consistently supports a **short (~15–20 min, <30 min) early-afternoon nap** for reducing sleepiness and improving performance (Hayashi & Hori, Hiroshima Univ.; Takahashi, JNIOSH). MHLW's 2014 sleep guideline endorses naps of ≤30 min in early afternoon. Osaka City Univ./RIKEN fatigue research (Watanabe, Tanaka) uses **30-min n-back blocks** as a standard "fatigue-inducing" dose: 30 min of demanding working-memory work is enough to produce measurable mental fatigue.

### Cited Findings
- **Hayashi et al. (1999a/b, Japan, university students):** A **20-min nap soon after lunch (from 12:20)** reduced sleepiness, improved subjective performance, and raised alpha-wave-based arousal. **Hayashi & Hori (1998):** a 20-min nap from ~14:00 also improved *objective* performance on computer tasks (logical reasoning, addition, visual detection, auditory vigilance). [MODERATE, small student samples; seen only via secondary citation] — cited in [Fujino et al. 2024, Frontiers in Sleep](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2024.1361886/full)
- Hayashi & Hori also wrote a 2007 Japanese review, "A short nap as a countermeasure against afternoon sleepiness," in *Physiological Psychology and Psychophysiology* (contents not seen). — [CiNii record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1010000781873552784?lang=en)
- **Takahashi et al. (2004, Ergonomics, JNIOSH):** A **15-min post-lunch nap** was tested at a worksite, in 8 factory workers over a 3-week protocol, measuring alertness, performance, and night sleep. [WEAK: n=8; detailed results not retrieved] — [Fundación MAPFRE record](https://documentacion.fundacionmapfre.org/documentacion/publico/en/bib/57606.do?queryId=64)
- **Takahashi (2003, Sleep Medicine Reviews, review):** Short naps of **< 30 min** can counter sleep-loss effects and help with shift work. Keeping under 30 min avoids sleep inertia. — [record](https://stafforini.com/works/takahashi-2003-role-prescribed-napping/); also cited in [Fujino 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2024.1361886/full)
- **JNIOSH night-shift nap studies:** Two naps during simulated night shifts reduced sleepiness and fatigue and maintained performance ([J Occup Health 60(2):172](https://academic.oup.com/joh/article/60/2/172/7250946)). A 2-h nap was tested in 12-h simulated night work ([Industrial Health 2021, Hokkaido Univ. repository](https://eprints.lib.hokudai.ac.jp/dspace/handle/2115/83779)). Takahashi (2015) also reviewed the chronically short sleep of Japanese workers ([NIPH J 64(1)](https://www.niph.go.jp/journal/data/64-1/201564010004.pdf)).
- **Fujino et al. (2024, Frontiers in Sleep, Japan, RCT crossover, n=20 men aged 23–62 with suspected "brain fatigue"; Toyota-funded):** A 30-min post-lunch rest (13:10–13:40) on a nap chair vs. an office chair. Nap chair: 19.0 vs. 7.6 min of sleep, no REM, KSS sleepiness −1.5 vs. −0.1 (P=0.0004), higher HF-HRV (parasympathetic). However, the **Uchida-Kraepelin performance test did not differ** (5.9 vs. 5.5 points, P=0.68). [MODERATE; industry-funded, men only] — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2024.1361886/full)
- **MHLW 「健康づくりのための睡眠指針2014」:** When night sleep is insufficient, a **short nap of ≤30 min in the early afternoon** is effective for improving work efficiency affected by sleepiness. Longer naps cause sleep inertia. Commentaries suggest ~15–20 min as a practical target. [Official guideline; seen via secondary HR sources only] — [jinjibu.jp](https://jinjibu.jp/keyword/detl/658/); [jinjibu.jp survey article](https://jinjibu.jp/article/detl/hr-survey/1049/)
- **Osaka City Univ. / RIKEN fatigue research (Watanabe, Tanaka et al.):**
  - Tanaka et al. (2012, *Behav Brain Funct* 8:48, Japan, n=18 healthy men, crossover). Four consecutive **30-min** 0-back or 2-back sessions were used to induce mental fatigue, with EEG before and after. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1744-9081-8-48)
  - Tanaka et al. (*Behav Brain Funct* 7:46, n=19): a **30-min 2-back** session induced fatigue measured by the advanced trail-making test, subjective scales, and ECG autonomic indices. — [BBF 7:46](https://behavioralandbrainfunctions.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1744-9081-7-46)
  - Shigihara, Tanaka et al. (2013, MEG): **30 min** of 0-back vs. 2-back produced two different "types" of mental fatigue, with distinct oscillatory brain signatures. — [PMC3562167](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3562167/)
  - Related EEG paper on the central-nervous-system effects of mental fatigue: [PMC3804027](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3804027/)

### Inferences
- Japanese lab work treats **~30 min of intensive cognitive work as sufficient to produce measurable mental fatigue**. This does not mean 30 min is a ceiling for productive work; it shows fatigue accumulates measurably well before the 60-min break point.
- For an app: a ~60-min session fits Japanese guidelines, but for highly demanding work (working-memory-heavy tasks), fatigue signals appear at ~30 min. This supports a mid-session micro-pause, matching MHLW's 小休止.
- An optional "power nap" feature should default to **15–20 min, early afternoon (≈12:30–15:00), and never >30 min**. Japanese experimental (Hayashi, Takahashi) and official (MHLW 2014) sources agree on this.
- Fujino 2024 suggests naps reliably reduce *sleepiness* even when short-term *performance* tests show no change. Claims in the app should focus on alertness, not guaranteed productivity.

### Gaps
- Original Hayashi & Hori 1998/1999 abstracts (exact n, effect sizes) were not retrieved; only secondary summaries were seen.
- Takahashi 2004 numeric results (alertness and performance effect sizes) were not retrieved.
- The MHLW 2023 「健康づくりのための睡眠ガイド2023」 nap wording was not verified.
- **Inemuri** (居眠り) culture: no academic source was retrieved in this pass (e.g., Brigitte Steger's anthropological work). It is not covered here.
- No JNIOSH study specifically on daytime *cognitive* work-break schedules (e.g., 50/10 vs. 90/15) was found.

## Q3. China: EEG sustained-attention and time-on-task fatigue, occupational standards, and 午休 (midday nap) research

### Takeaway
Chinese EEG studies are mostly small. One classroom EEG study (n=8) found mental fatigue lowest at ~19 min and back to baseline at ~43 min, and it recommended ~45-min lessons. Broader time-on-task EEG literature (incl. Chinese co-authors) shows alpha rising early (often saturating within ~1 h) and theta rising steadily. Chinese midday-nap epidemiology (elderly n=2,214; children n≈3,000) links habitual napping with better cognition and academic performance, but it is observational. **No Chinese national standard (GB/GBZ) prescribing VDT work–break intervals was found.**

### Cited Findings
- **Huang, Huan, Zou, Wang, Gao, Zheng (2024, *Cognitive Neurodynamics* 18(6):3477–3489, China: Southern Medical Univ., Fudan, Tsinghua, CAS):** Group-synchronized dry-electrode EEG in a real class (80 min, no break; n=10 recorded, **8 analyzed**). Fatigue index: (θ+α)/β and γ/δ. Mental fatigue reached its minimum at **~18.9 min**, the "effective attention span." It then rose back to the initial level at **~43.1 min**. Authors recommend lessons of **~45 min**. **[WEAK]**: n=8, only one subject matched the group pattern, EEG-only, and the abstract's wording differs from the results section. — [PMC11655751](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11655751)
- **Chinese EEG study (Journal of UESTC, 2019):** n=50 completed 3 different long mental tasks. Results: rising subjective fatigue, longer reaction times on complex arithmetic, more switching-task errors, and **reduced P300 amplitude**, interpreted as central fatigue. [MODERATE; exact minute of onset not reported in the retrieved summary] — [J UESTC 2019](https://www.juestc.uestc.edu.cn/article/doi/10.3969/j.issn.1001-0548.2019.04.020)
- **Chinese review (第二军医大学学报 2019):** Measurement of mental fatigue (脑力疲劳) is split into symptom-based, psychological/behavioral, and physiological/biochemical methods. There is **no unified standard**. — [Acad J Second Mil Med Univ 2019 40(1):79–85](https://html.rhhz.net/dejydxxb/html/2019/1/20180168.htm)
- **Chinese safety journal (中国安全科学学报 2024):** EEG test of operator monitoring behavior over long sessions (details not retrieved). — [CAST journals PDF](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/zgaqkxxb/EN/PDF/10.16265/j.cnki.issn1003-3033.2024.09.0997)
- **Time-on-task EEG with Chinese co-authors (Liu, Zhang, Cong, Parviainen et al., 2020, *Front Hum Neurosci* 14:207; Jyväskylä/Dalian collaboration):** A 2 h 20 min selective-attention task plus an 80-min sustained-attention EEG dataset with a motivation manipulation at 60–80 min. This frames time-on-task decline as partly **motivational**. — [Jyväskylä thesis](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_72881)
- **Related (non-Chinese) context:** In an 80-min sustained-attention EEG task, performance and motivation fell **early and plateaued** well before 60 min (Reteig et al., Cortex 2019, Netherlands) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/501544.full.pdf). In long tasks, parietal alpha rises fast and saturates by ~1 h while frontal theta keeps rising (simulated-flight EEG-fNIRS) — [PMC10036528](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10036528/)
- **Chinese VDT work patterns (Gu Ligang et al., 2008, 《职业与健康》, Beijing Univ. of Technology, n=206):** Average daily VDT work **7.03 h**, and continuous VDT use **3.63 h**, far beyond Japan's 1-h limit. The authors call for health management of VDT workers. [MODERATE, survey] — [BJUT repository](https://ir.bjut.edu.cn/item/163633)
- **Chinese national standard:** Searches did not locate a GB/GBZ standard with explicit continuous-VDT-work and break limits. Chinese-language summaries cite the Japanese MHLW 2002 rule as the reference. — (negative finding; see [PMC6499340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499340) as the commonly cited reference)
- **午休 and cognition, elderly (Cai H. et al., 2021, *General Psychiatry* 34:e100361, Wuhu, Anhui, China):** n=**2,214** Han Chinese aged ≥60. 1,534 nappers vs. 680 non-nappers. Nappers had better cognition (MMSE), especially **orientation, language, memory**. Commentator Phyllis Zee noted the benefit seems tied to *planned* naps, while sleepiness-driven napping in older adults is a risk marker. [MODERATE; cross-sectional; nap defined as 5 min–2 h after lunch per secondary coverage, not verified] — [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/afternoon-napping-associated-better-cognition-elderly-study-shows); [SWJPCC summary](https://www.swjpcc.com/articles-from-other-journals/2021/2/2/relationship-between-afternoon-napping-and-cognitive-functio.html)
  - Note: the assignment mentions a "2023 Gen Psychiatry" study. The Cai et al. paper found is **2021**. No separate 2023 Chinese Gen Psychiatry napping study was found.
- **午休 in children (Liu J. et al., 2019, *Sleep* 42(9) zsz126, China Jintan Cohort):** 3,819 schoolchildren characterized (≈2,928 with Grade-6 outcomes). Napping ≥3×/week was associated with higher happiness, grit, and self-control, fewer behavior problems, higher IQ, and a **7.6% increase in Grade-6 academic performance**. [MODERATE; observational] — [Ovid abstract](https://www.ovid.com/journals/slep/abstract/10.1093/sleep/zsz126~midday-napping-in-children-associations-between-nap); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/afternoon-nap-children-behavior-14142/)
- **Same cohort, adolescents (Ji, Liu et al., 2018, Univ. of Delaware / Jintan):** Teens who nap had better attention, nonverbal reasoning, and spatial memory. The "sweet spot" was **30–60 min**, and naps **>1 h** were associated with worse performance. [MODERATE; observational; press-release level] — [UDaily](https://www.udel.edu/udaily/2018/april/xiaopeng-ji-napping-neurocognitive-function/)

### Inferences
- Chinese nap evidence is **epidemiological (large n, but correlational)**, while Japanese nap evidence is **experimental (small n, causal)**. Together they support a habitual early-afternoon nap. Note the conflict on duration: Chinese adolescent data suggest 30–60 min is best, while Japanese/MHLW guidance says ≤30 min to avoid inertia. The likely difference is age group and habituation (Chinese students nap daily as an institution).
- Chinese EEG data, though weak, are compatible with the idea that **attention peaks within ~20 min and fatigue becomes measurable by ~40–45 min** of passive, continuous input (lecture). Active, self-paced work (as in a focus app) likely sustains longer, but this is not directly tested.
- Chinese workers surveyed in 2008 did ~3.6 h continuous VDT work with no standard requiring breaks. A focus app that enforces hourly breaks addresses a real gap in China.

### Gaps
- No Chinese GB/GBZ standard on VDT breaks confirmed. Check GBZ/T 2xx ergonomic standards and 国家卫健委 guidance directly.
- No Chinese EEG study giving a specific "fatigue onset minute" for adult desk work (60–120 min) was retrieved in full. Chinese driving-fatigue literature (often 90–120 min protocols) was not covered.
- No Chinese controlled work–break schedule experiment (e.g., 工间休息 / 工间操 effects) was found.
- Cai 2021 full-text adjustments and nap-duration breakdowns were not verified.

## Q4. Studies in Chinese / Japanese students on study-session length and Pomodoro (番茄工作法)

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed Chinese or Japanese controlled study of Pomodoro or optimal study-session length was found. Chinese-language material on 番茄工作法 is popular/methodological (25 + 5 min, long break after 4 cycles). The only EEG-based Chinese "session length" evidence is the weak classroom study suggesting ~45-min lessons.

### Cited Findings
- Chinese sources describe Pomodoro as **25 min focus + 5 min break**, with a longer break after 4 cycles. This is a practitioner heuristic, not based on Chinese controlled trials. [WEAK, popular sources] — [jibble.io (Chinese)](https://www.jibble.io/zh-hans/文章/番茄工作法); [CSDN article](https://deepseek.csdn.net/6a1cdf5b10ee7a33f27696bc.html)
- The only academic Pomodoro comparison surfaced, "Self-Regulated vs. Pomodoro vs. Flowtime break-taking among students," is **Maastricht (Netherlands)**, not East Asian. — [PMC12292963](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12292963/)
- The Chinese classroom EEG study recommends lesson length of **~45 min**, with the effective span at ~19 min. [WEAK, n=8] — [PMC11655751](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11655751)

### Inferences
- The East Asian school system itself uses ~40–45 min class periods with ~10-min breaks plus a long midday break/nap in China. This is institutional practice rather than tested optimization, and its numbers sit between Pomodoro (25/5) and the MHLW work rule (60/10–15).

### Gaps
- CNKI-indexed Chinese master's/thesis studies on 番茄工作法 and student efficiency likely exist but were not accessible via the search tools. Quality is likely low.
- No Japanese study on ポモドーロ・テクニック or 学習時間 / 休憩 for students was found in this pass.
- Exact class-period lengths in Chinese/Japanese schools (e.g., Japanese 45/50-min periods) were not sourced here.

## Q5. Agreement / disagreement with Western findings on session length (~50–90 min) and breaks

### Takeaway
East Asian evidence **broadly agrees** with Western guidance. Japan's official 60 min on / 10–15 min off (+ micro-pauses) is nearly identical to US DoL 1997 (60/15 for high demand) and UK HSE (50–60 min then 5–10 min). Japanese and Chinese lab studies show measurable fatigue after ~30–45 min of intensive or passive tasks, which argues for micro-breaks inside a 60-min block rather than for longer 90-min blocks. Nap guidance (≤30 min, early afternoon) matches Western power-nap research. One divergence: Chinese cohort data favour somewhat longer habitual naps (30–60 min) in adolescents.

### Cited Findings
- **Japan 2002/2019:** 60 min max → 10–15 min break, +1–2 小休止 of ~1–2 min. **US DoL 1997:** 60 min → 15 min (high demand) or 120 min → 10 min (moderate). **UK HSE 1992:** 50–60 min → 5–10 min preferred over 120 → 15. — [PMC6499340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499340); [HSE FAQ](https://www.hse.gov.uk/contact/faqs/vdubreaks.htm)
- **Other countries:** Sweden suggested a 1–2 h continuous-VDT ceiling ([NIOSH review of VDT rest-break literature](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/226632)). Italy requires a 15-min break or task change every 120 min of display work ([Univ. of Bologna](https://site.unibo.it/@@multilingual-selector/7476c4bf328a466eb32b60de13a71038/en)).
- **Micro-breaks beat long blocks:** Balci et al. 2003 (15-min work + micro-breaks beat 60/10 and 30/5), cited by Japanese researchers — [PMC6499340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499340)
- **Fatigue time course:** Japan: 30 min of 2-back is enough to induce fatigue ([Tanaka 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1744-9081-8-48)). China: classroom EEG fatigue back to baseline at ~43 min ([Huang 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11655751)). West: performance drops early and plateaus within an 80-min task ([Reteig 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/501544.full.pdf)).
- **Naps:** Japan: 15–20 min ([Hayashi, Takahashi via Fujino 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2024.1361886/full)); MHLW: ≤30 min early afternoon ([jinjibu](https://jinjibu.jp/keyword/detl/658/)); China adolescents: 30–60 min best, >60 worse ([UDaily](https://www.udel.edu/udaily/2018/april/xiaopeng-ji-napping-neurocognitive-function/)); China elderly: napping associated with better cognition ([Cai 2021](https://mdedge.com/content/afternoon-napping-associated-better-cognition-elderly-study-shows)).

### Inferences (design implications for a ~1-hour focus app)
- **Default 60 min work → 10–15 min break** is directly supported by Japan's MHLW guideline and matches US/UK guidance. It sits at the short end of the Western "50–90 min" ultradian-style range.
- **Add 1–2 micro-pauses (≈1–2 min, or 20–60 s eye-rest) at ~20 and ~40 min.** This mirrors MHLW 小休止, the ~19-min attention peak in the Chinese EEG study, the ~30-min fatigue induction in Japanese n-back studies, and the micro-break advantage in Balci 2003.
- **Daily cap:** MHLW's note cites ~5 h/day (300 min) of intensive screen work as desirable. That works out to roughly 4–5 one-hour sessions per day.
- **Optional nap mode:** 15–20 min (max 30), between ~12:30 and 15:00. This is backed by Japanese RCTs and the MHLW 2014 guideline. Chinese cohort data support daily 午休 as a habit.
- **Evidence-strength caveat:** None of these numbers comes from a large RCT that optimized session length for knowledge work. The 60-min rule is a precautionary occupational-health consensus, and the EEG minute values are small-sample.

### Gaps
- No direct East Asian head-to-head trial of 25/5 vs. 50/10 vs. 60/15 vs. 90/20 for cognitive work was found.
- South Korea (KOSHA) and Taiwan (勞動部) VDT break rules were not researched.
- The Western ultradian (~90 min, Kleitman BRAC) evidence was not examined here; other researchers presumably cover it.
